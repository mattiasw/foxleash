# Code of conduct

Foxleash is a small project that people use and improve together. This page says what the
maintainer expects of everyone taking part, so that the project stays a place where people can
get help and do useful work.

## What is expected

- Be respectful and patient. People differ in experience, background and first language.
- Assume good intent, and ask when something is unclear before you take offence.
- Keep to the topic of the issue, pull request or discussion you are in.
- Give feedback on the work, not on the person.
- Accept that the maintainer decides what goes into the project, and that the answer can be no.

## What is not accepted

- Harassment of any kind, including repeated unwanted contact.
- Personal attacks, insults and demeaning comments.
- Discrimination or hateful language aimed at a person or a group.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as a home address, without their consent.
- Sustained disruption of a conversation, such as repeating the same demand or posting off-topic
  or spam content.

## Where it applies

This applies in every space of the project: issues, pull requests, discussions, comments and
any other place where people take part in Foxleash. It also applies when you act for the project
in public.

## What the maintainer may do

The maintainer may edit, hide or remove a comment, issue or pull request that breaks this code,
lock a conversation, and block an account from the project's spaces. The maintainer decides
what is appropriate in each case, and may act without a warning when the behaviour is serious.

## How to report

Report a comment, issue or pull request that breaks this code with GitHub's "Report content"
item, in the `...` menu of that comment, issue or pull request. To report a person, the
maintainer included, use "Block or report" on their GitHub profile. Both kinds of report go to
GitHub's staff. The maintainer moderates the project's spaces.

To reach the maintainer privately, write to <mattias@mattiaswallander.com>.

Report a security vulnerability as [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) describes instead, not in a
public place.
